What time is it in your life?

LifeMeter maps your life onto a single day. If you've lived 45% of the years you can expect, it's 10:54 AM in your life, and most of your day is still ahead. LifeMeter counts what's left in weeks, summers and the moments that matter, so you can spend them on purpose.

It's a gentle wake-up call, not a countdown to dread: every week is a fresh box.

★ YOUR LIFE CLOCK
• Your life mapped onto a 24-hour day: "It's 10:54 AM in your life."
• Weeks, days, hours and live seconds left, shown as a slideshow you can tap through
• A bold progress bar of the life you've lived, with a pink "now" marker

★ YOUR LIFE IN WEEKS
• Every week of your life as a square, one row per year, with this week marked
• Add a short memory to any week and watch your life fill with colour

★ MAKE IT COUNT
• Your one thing: the one thing you most want to do, kept beside your count
• A bucket list: tick things off as you live them, or pin one as your one thing
• The tail end: how many summers, weekends, full moons, books and sunrises are ahead
• Your people: "About 50 more visits with Mum". Time together, counted kindly
• Milestones such as 10,000 days old, so you can celebrate them

★ KEEP IT IN SIGHT
• Home-screen widgets: a countdown (slideshow or one unit), your life in weeks, and the Life Clock. They resize down to two cells, so they fit side by side
• Set your life grid as your lock-screen wallpaper
• A motivational quote every day

★ SHARE IT
• One tap turns your Life Clock, your weeks or a milestone into a card for your stories: "What time is it in your life?"

★ HONEST AND PRIVATE
• Life expectancy for 217 countries from World Bank / UN World Population Prospects data, adjusted by five optional, evidence-based habits
• Shown as a range, never a date, with the method and sources inside the app
• No account, no ads, no tracking. Your answers stay on your phone
• Light and dark themes

Set up in about 30 seconds: your birthday, your country, and you're in.

LifeMeter gives an estimate from population data. It is not a prediction and not medical advice.

Inspired by Tim Urban's "Your Life in Weeks" and Oliver Burkeman's "Four Thousand Weeks".
